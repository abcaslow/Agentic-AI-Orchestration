# Defining Terms: Socialism and Capitalism — United with Code (Blog)

**Source:** https://smartnewdeal.com/defining-terms-socialism-and-capitalism/
**Author:** Bernie Wolzak | August 11, 2019 | Category: Ideology
**Captured:** 2026-07-08

The post examines the emotionally charged terms "socialist/capitalist" and "socialism/capitalism," motivated by the 2019–2020 political climate (the Trump campaign labeling Democratic candidates "socialists," and factions on the left treating "socialist" as a badge of honor and "capitalism" with suspicion). Its long-term aim is to introduce a 21st-century "3rd Way" — "Techie Democratic Capitalist."

**Conclusion up front:** socialism and capitalism are not mutually exclusive. America already lives in a hybrid socialist/capitalist society, as do all EU nations. Adopting, e.g., Bernie Sanders' Medicare for All would not make America "socialist" — it would be a socialist healthcare system within an otherwise capitalist nation.

**Two flavors of Socialism:** (1) Soviet-Style Socialism (former USSR — what Churchill criticized); (2) Scandinavian Socialism (Norway, Denmark, Sweden — what Bernie Sanders embraces; some conservatives dispute calling it real "socialism").

**Four flavors of Capitalism:** (1) Crony Capitalism; (2) Laissez Faire Capitalism; (3) Democratic Capitalism (Pete Buttigieg's form); (4) Techie Democratic Capitalism — as Internet technologies evolve (cloud computing, 5G, IoT, big data, AI), they offer an opportunity to empower individuals worldwide within this framework (to be detailed in a future post).

The post references a Christian Science Monitor article by Harry Bruinius on how socialism is viewed in a Trump-friendly Pennsylvania county, a C-SPAN interview, and a Yahoo! Finance graph rating 2020 Democratic candidates on a socialist/capitalist spectrum (noting Elizabeth Warren "insists she's a capitalist"). Conclusion: the US has been a hybrid of capitalism and socialism at least since the FDR administration (New Deal, FHA, TVA, NRA). The author self-identifies as a "techie democratic capitalist" and suggests John Delaney, Pete Buttigieg, and Andrew Yang would understand the term.
