# Categories of Software Intelligence — United with Code (Blog)

**Author:** Bernie Wolzak | August 20, 2019 | Category: Technology

In the book *The Big Nine*, Amy Webb lists three forms of Artificial Intelligence: (1) Artificial Narrow Intelligence (p. 53); (2) Artificial General Intelligence (p. 53); (3) Artificial Super Intelligence (p. 143).

There are other overlapping terms used to classify AI. Three common ones used interchangeably: (1) **Expert Systems** (Webb's Artificial Narrow Intelligence), (2) **Machine Learning**, (3) **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**. All of these different, overlapping terms create confusion.

**Expert Systems (Artificial Narrow Intelligence):** designed for a very specific, targeted AI application in a field such as medicine, finance, law or cyber-security. An expert system is a computer program that uses AI technologies to simulate the judgment and behavior of a human or organization with expert knowledge in a particular field.

**Machine Learning and AI (broader classifications):** Machine Learning and AI systems look for patterns and perform pattern matching.

Examples cited:
- **IBM Watson** and its AI-based Natural Language Processing system (Unstructured Information Management Architecture is its keystone).
- **Google's AI offerings:** search autocomplete (and YouTube), GMAIL sentence completion, translate.google.com (free AI translation), ad ranking algorithms, DeepMind's AlphaZero (defeats chess masters, plus shogi and Go; open-source counterpart: Stockfish), Boston Dynamics robotics, and TensorFlow (AI/ML library).
- **Apple Siri and Amazon Alexa** voice-recognition AI (Alexa can steer users to paid Amazon suppliers — a major revenue stream).
- **Mediocre/bad AI:** AT&T and Verizon automated directory services — frustrating, often failing to supply the desired number ("Google AI wins; Verizon and AT&T AI loses").
- **AWS AI toolkit** including facial recognition (Rekognition).
- **Facial recognition + AI trends:** a 195-gigapixel photo of Shanghai (BigPixel) demonstrating zoom to street-level detail; Japan planned to use facial recognition + AI for security at the 2020 Olympics.
- **Open-source NLP:** Apache OpenNLP (a machine-learning toolkit for natural language text).
