# Automation vs. Artificial Intelligence — United with Code (Blog)

**Source:** https://smartnewdeal.com/automation-vs-artificial-intelligence/
**Author:** Bernie Wolzak | August 21, 2019 | Category: Technology
**Captured:** 2026-07-08

While Artificial Intelligence is the centerpiece topic in information technology, its less sexy counterpart — "Automation" — is having just as big an impact on our economy, on jobs, and on our civilization overall.

We encounter automation everywhere: grocery self-checkout lanes, airport check-in kiosks, and McDonald's ordering kiosks. These routine tasks (tallying items, checking in a passenger, ordering a meal) have been transformed through plain vanilla automation — often no AI involved — and shifted onto customers as "self-service."

The post cites Andrew Yang (Chapter Six of *The War on Normal People*): the important distinction is not white-collar vs. blue-collar or cognitive vs. manual, but **routine vs. non-routine**. Routine jobs of all stripes are most under threat from AI and automation — including doctors, lawyers, accountants, wealth advisors, traders, journalists — while non-routine work (per a Gartner post by Craig Roth) is where developed economies generate the most value.

The author describes automation as the primary focus of his career since 2015. Since 1994 he worked with Cisco networking products, automating with a patchwork of tools: Expect, Perl, BASH, command-line SNMP — mostly automating human interaction over SSH terminal sessions. Around 2008, Cisco released platforms (Nexus, UCS) with NETCONF and REST interfaces enabling pure automation, as did Juniper and Arista. Alongside came AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure/Windows-365, VMWare virtualization, and Cisco private/hybrid cloud — all centered on automation ("software defined networking," "infrastructure as code"), driving IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS.

By 2019 his automation toolkit included: Python; open-source IDEs (Jupyter Notebooks, PyCharm); JSON/XML tools (jmespath, xpath); REST and Postman; NETCONF/RESTCONF; data modeling languages (YANG & OpenConfig); Puppet/Chef/Ansible/SaltStack; Splunk/ELK Stack; git and GitHub. He calls this transition "From CLI to API."

**Trade-off:** these tools create a new challenge — complexity and support burden. To make automation "scale," embrace (1) code sharing via GitHub and (2) flexibility/agility to migrate to more scalable solutions as they mature. A student in the Philippines put it: "Learn to automate or be terminated." Closing note: when AWS had an outage from a glitch in its scalable automation tools, it solved the glitch with... MORE AUTOMATION.
