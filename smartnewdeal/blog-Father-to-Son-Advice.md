A father encourages his son to pursue an extracurricular project. Since the son's high school is right outside Washington DC and has a "Washington Program" that takes him into DC every Wednesday, he is well positioned to get involved with the 2020 Presidential election. The father likes the idea of investigating the impact of technology in the government of the future and rating how each 2020 candidate grasps the topic — with Andrew Yang a great starting point (Yang argues "The US Government is 24 Years Behind on Tech").

The post discusses candidate age groups (via a Time magazine article):
- **Age Group #1 — Older candidates (70s):** Bernie Sanders (77), Joe Biden (76), Donald Trump (73), Elizabeth Warren (70).
- **Age Group #2 — Younger candidates:** Andrew Yang (44), Pete Buttigieg (37).

Age spreads: Sanders–Buttigieg 40 years; Warren–Buttigieg 33 years; Sanders–Yang 33 years; Warren–Yang 26 years. Yang and Buttigieg grew up with Internet and smartphone technology (Yang is a Silicon Valley executive), while the oldest candidates are at best peripheral users. The father argues the younger candidates better grasp the megatrends of On-Demand Cloud Computing, High-Speed 5G Public Networks, and Artificial Intelligence.

Two recommended books (bought for father and son to read together):
- **The Big Nine** by Amy Webb — the nine corporations (Amazon, Google, Facebook, Tencent, Baidu, Alibaba, Microsoft, IBM, Apple) as the "new gods of AI" (six American, three Chinese); examines their impact on civilization and government.
- **The War on Normal People** by Andrew Yang — the potentially negative effects of robotics and AI on millions of jobs.
