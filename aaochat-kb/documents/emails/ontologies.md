An ontology in artificial intelligence is a formal, machine-readable
specification of a domain's concepts, properties, and relationships that
acts as a structured semantic framework for reasoning and data
integration. [1
<https://medium.com/@nfigay/what-is-an-ontology-in-the-artificial-intelligence-context-b0f935d34aab>
, 2
<https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/machine-learning/introduction-to-ontologies/>
, 3 <https://www.puppygraph.com/blog/ai-ontology>]
Core Components

 - *Classes (Concepts):* Categories or groups of similar objects (e.g.,
 *Customer*, *Product*, *Order*).
 - *Instances (Individuals):* Specific, real-world examples of a class
 (e.g., *Customer #1042*).
 - *Properties (Attributes):* Characteristics or data values assigned to
 classes and instances.
 - *Relationships:* How different entities connect to one another (e.g.,
 *places*, *contains*).
 - *Axioms and Constraints:* Codified rules and logical conditions that
 govern valid states and operations.[1
 <https://www.dawiso.com/glossary/ontology-in-ai>, 2
 <https://www.salesforce.com/blog/design-what-is-ontology/>, 3
 <https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/machine-learning/introduction-to-ontologies/>
 ]

How Ontologies Relate to AI

 - *Grounding LLMs and Reducing Hallucinations:* Large Language Models
 (LLMs) are probabilistic and often ungrounded. Ontologies act as
 "guardrails" and a world model, forcing AI to operate within explicit
 business logic and domain rules rather than statistical guessing. [1
 <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YuaxC6sEA5I>, 2
 <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sir59K8ZDPU&vl=en&t=26>, 3
 <https://datavid.com/blog/what-is-an-ontology>]
 - *Neurosymbolic AI:* They bridge statistical neural networks with
 symbolic AI, using standard web logic languages like OWL (Web Ontology
 Language) and RDFS (Resource Description Framework Schema) to enable
 logical inference and verification. [1
 <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Z4ie_MQOTw>, 2
 <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sir59K8ZDPU&vl=en&t=26>]
 - *The Relationship to Knowledge Graphs:* An ontology is the *schema* (the
 rules, classes, and allowed relationships), whereas a knowledge graph is
 the *populated data* (the actual instances tied to that schema). [1
 <https://datavid.com/blog/what-is-an-ontology>, 2
 <https://www.dawiso.com/glossary/ontology-in-ai>]
 - *Semantic Search and RAG:* Ontologies power semantic search by mapping
 synonyms and equivalent concepts (e.g., linking *"heart attack"* to
*"myocardial
 infarction"*), making Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and GraphRAG
 systems far more accurate. [1
 <https://www.puppygraph.com/blog/ai-ontology>, 2
 <https://datavid.com/blog/what-is-an-ontology>]
 - *Agentic AI & Tool Selection:* Autonomous AI agents use ontologies as
 machine-readable contracts to understand what tools, APIs, or database
 actions are permissible for a specific sub-task. [1
 <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Z4ie_MQOTw>, 2
 <https://www.puppygraph.com/blog/ai-ontology>]
 - *Explainability and Governance:* In regulated industries, ontologies
 provide a transparent, traceable decision path. When an AI makes a choice,
 its reasoning can be audited step-by-step against formal domain rules. [1
 <https://www.progress.com/blogs/the-resurgence-of-ontologies-ontology-driven-ai>
 , 2 <https://www.puppygraph.com/blog/ai-ontology>] Would you like to explore *how to build a basic ontology* using tools like
Protégé, or look at how ontologies integrate with *Enterprise RAG pipelines*
